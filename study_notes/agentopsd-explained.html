A local gap is not sequential credit

AgentOPSD turns a self-distillation log-probability gap into a running Bayesian belief about whether the episode will succeed — and pays each turn by how much it moved that belief.

MotivationEverything in the trajectory gets paid the same

ALFWorld hands you one bit at the very end: did you put the clean mug in the coffee machine or not. Fifty turns of decisions, one scalar.

GRPO's answer is to sample a group of G trajectories for the same task, standardize their binary rewards into a group-relative advantage, and then broadcast that single number to every token in the trajectory. If the episode succeeded, every action in it gets pushed up equally — including the three turns you spent opening the wrong cabinet. If it failed, every action gets pushed down equally — including the correct reasoning that got you 90% of the way there.

The paper's framing is that this is not just imprecise, it is increasingly imprecise. The longer the horizon, the more decisions one broadcast scalar has to cover, and the more of the update is noise. Their Figure 1(b) measures this directly: regress per-sub-task success on the mean number of turns those episodes take, and read off how many success points you lose per extra turn. GRPO loses 2.91; RLSD loses 3.59.

The complementary line: self-distillation gaps

A separate body of work gets denser supervision without a critic. You run the same model twice on the same tokens the student just generated — once plain, once with privileged context that is only available at training time. The difference in log-probability is a free, dense, per-token signal about which tokens the privileged view considers characteristic.

Here the privileged context c⁺ is a retrieved skill — a short text description of subgoals and action patterns for this task family, pulled by keyword matching from the SkillBank of SkillRL. It appears only in the teacher forward pass during training. It is never in the rollout prompt and never at inference.

The two mismatches AgentOPSD claims to fix

  • Granularity. The gap is per-token, but agentic environments don't respond per token. Many tokens jointly form one action, and the environment only speaks at turn boundaries. A token-level signal fragments a single decision.
  • Isolation. Even the step-aware prior work (StepOPSD) scores each turn by its own local log-ratio. But whether a turn matters depends on what already happened. "Take the mug" is decisive when you have no mug and no idea where one is. The identical action is near-worthless as evidence once you've already established the mug is in cabinet 1.
The thesis in one sentence. The credit of a turn is not its local signal, but how much that signal revises the estimated probability of eventual success. That framing makes credit a Bayesian belief update, and belief updates are naturally recursive.

The machineryFive equations, in order

Nothing here needs a value network, a reward model, or a single extra rollout. The entire cost over GRPO is one additional forward pass per trajectory.

1 · What we'd want, but can't have

Eq. 3 — the ideal Bayes factor
logit p(C | s_k, a_k) − logit p(C | s_k) = log [ p(a_k | s_k, C) / p(a_k | s_k, ¬C) ]

C is the event "this trajectory eventually succeeds." The left side is exactly the change in belief about success caused by taking action a_k. The right side says that change equals the log-ratio of how likely a_k is under success-behavior versus failure-behavior. Both outcome-conditional distributions are unavailable, so this is a target, not a computation.

2 · The computable stand-in

Eq. 4–6 — token contrast, summed to a turn
h_k,t = (s_k, y_k,<t) ← student context h⁺_k,t = (s_k, c⁺, y_k,<t) ← teacher context, skill injected δ_k,t = log π_θ(y_k,t | h⁺_k,t) − log π_θ(y_k,t | h_k,t) e_k = Σ_t δ_k,t = log [ π_θ(a_k | s_k, c⁺) / π_θ(a_k | s_k) ]

Same parameters θ in both branches — this is self-distillation, not a bigger teacher. Both branches score the tokens the student already sampled, so the sum telescopes into a clean action-level log-ratio. This is the fix for mismatch #1: sum over exactly the tokens of one action, then stop, because that's where the environment answers. The whole thing is detached (sg[·]) — no gradient flows through it.

Read Appendix A.1 before you trust the Bayes story. The approximation needs two assumptions: (A1) the skill-conditioned branch really is success-conditional behaviour, and (A2) the unconditioned marginal is failure-dominated, which only holds in the limit where success is rare (ρk → 0). Late in training on ALFWorld, success is common, so A2 is plainly violated. The authors hedge accordingly: under A1 alone e_k is pointwise mutual information, positive exactly when the action raises the posterior success probability — and the correction term is monotone, so the sign and ranking survive even when the magnitude doesn't. AgentOPSD only ever uses sign and ranking. Calling B_k a probability is a convenient fiction; it's relative support.

3 · The recursion — this is the actual contribution

Eq. 8–9 — belief state in log-odds space
B_0 = clip(R̄, ε, 1−ε) c_0 = 0 ℓ_0 = logit(B_0) c_k = γ·c_{k−1} + e_k ℓ_k = logit(B_0) + c_k B_k = σ(ℓ_k) ΔB_k = B_k − B_{k−1} ≈ B_{k−1}(1 − B_{k−1}) · [ e_k − (1−γ)·c_{k−1} ]

Three things are doing work here, and the ablations separate all three.

  • The prior B₀ = R̄. The group success rate — GRPO's own group mean, reused. It is the MLE of task difficulty under a Binomial likelihood (Prop. 7), and it's free: you already computed it for the advantage. Crucially it also sets where on the sigmoid you start, and therefore how sensitive early turns are.
  • The decayed accumulator c_k. The net new evidence at turn k is e_k − (1−γ)c_{k−1}: your fresh evidence minus the decayed carry-over. At γ=1 this is the undiscounted log-likelihood-ratio accumulation of Wald's sequential test. At γ<1 the state is recency-weighted, so evidence from thirty turns ago stops pinning the support level.
  • The sensitivity gate B(1−B). This falls straight out of the sigmoid derivative. Evidence has maximum effect at B = ½ and vanishes as belief saturates toward 0 or 1. This is the fix for mismatch #2: the same e_k produces a large ΔB_k when the outcome is genuinely open, and a negligible one once the state already points somewhere.

4 · Aligning the revision with the verifier

Eq. 10 — outcome-aligned credit
q_k = sign(A_seq) · ΔB_k

Magnitude says how much the turn revised support. Sign records whether that revision agreed with what actually happened. In a successful episode, a turn that raised belief is consistent — amplify it. In a failed episode, a turn that raised belief was misleading confidence — the sign flip means the turns that dropped belief get the amplified penalty instead. Drop this sign and you keep only "where did belief move," losing all information about whether the move was right. The ablation costs 8.6 points.

5 · Reshaping, bounded

Eq. 11–12 — standardize, clip, mix
z_k = (q_k − μ_q) / (σ_q + ε) ← standardized WITHIN the trajectory w_k = clip(1 + b·z_k, 1−b, 1+b) ← b = 0.2 Ã_k = A_seq · [ (1−λ) + λ·w_k ] ← λ = 0.5 L = clipped-PPO surrogate with Ã_{κ(t)} per token + β·KL

Every token inherits its turn's Ã. Because z is standardized inside the trajectory it has mean zero, so this is a redistribution of advantage mass across turns, not an injection of extra magnitude.

Three properties matter and are all one-line proofs in Appendix A.2:

  • Bounded (Prop. 1): |Ã_k − A_seq| ≤ λb|A_seq|. At the paper's settings that's ±10%. This is a nudge, not a rewrite.
  • Sign-preserving (Prop. 2): the multiplier (1−λ)+λw_k ≥ 1−λb > 0, strictly positive. The belief signal can never reverse GRPO's update direction. The verifier owns the direction; belief only owns the magnitude. This is what makes the method safe to bolt on.
  • Recovers GRPO (Prop. 3): at λ=0 the gradient is identical to GRPO's. λ is a clean interpolation knob.
Where this sits relative to PPO. The authors put it plainly in §4.3: the belief state B plays the role of GAE's value baseline, and its per-turn revision ΔB plays the role of the TD signal — but with no learned value network, no reward model, and no extra rollouts. That's the trade they're proposing.

▪ verifier-derived — owns sign and scale ▪ teacher-derived — owns magnitude only, within ±10%
R̄ enters twice, and the paper doesn't discuss it. The group success rate sets both the belief's starting point and the advantage scale. So a hard task hands you a low B₀ — which sits in the high-sensitivity region of the B(1−B) gate, making early revisions large — and a large |A_seq| for whichever trajectories succeed. The two effects compound rather than being independent knobs. Drag the group-successes slider above and watch the Ã spread and the belief trace move together.

Reading the ledger

  • Turn 1 is the only turn punished. Its ΔB is negative, it standardizes to about z = −2.08, the multiplier clips hard to the floor 1−b = 0.8, and it ends up at roughly 0.70 against GRPO's flat 0.775. Still positive — Prop. 2 guarantees the sign can't flip — but visibly the weakest push in a successful episode.
  • Turn 4 is the pivot. Belief was sitting near 0.64, squarely in the high-sensitivity region of the B(1−B) gate, and e₄ = +1.4 pushes it to 0.88. That's a ΔB of +0.24, z ≈ +1.67, multiplier clipped to the ceiling 1+b = 1.2, top advantage.
  • Turns 7 and 8 get almost nothing — and that is on purpose. By turn 7 belief has saturated above 0.97, so B(1−B) ≈ 0.026 and even real evidence barely moves the state. The paper's own words: the same local gap that is decisive while the outcome is open becomes redundant once the accumulated state already points to an outcome.
  • The increments telescope. Σ ΔB_k = B_K − B₀ exactly (Prop. 5). Check it in the readout above.
Flip the toggle to R = 0 and watch what Eq. 10 does. The belief trace is identical — same evidence — but A_seq goes negative and q_k = sign(A_seq)·ΔB_k inverts the ranking. Turn 1, the only turn that lowered belief, becomes the most strongly penalized. In a failure, the turns that dragged support down are the ones you most want to push away from. (Contrived, of course: a genuinely failed episode would have produced different e_k in the first place. It isolates the mechanism, not the data.)
The saturation problem this example makes visible. ALFWorld allows up to 50 turns. Eight turns of mostly-positive evidence already saturate the belief. Because c_k converges to roughly e/(1−γ) ≈ 20e under sustained evidence, and the increment is e_k − (1−γ)c_{k−1}, the decay does bound the accumulator — but at a level well past saturation. In a 40-turn episode the last 30 turns would all sit near ΔB ≈ 0, standardize to below-average, and get downweighted toward 0.8×. The paper's horizon-robustness result (−0.54 pts/turn) says this empirically doesn't hurt; the mechanism doesn't obviously explain why. Slide γ down to 0.8 above and watch the trace stop pinning.

PipelineWhat actually runs, per training step

Reconstructed from Algorithm 1 (Appendix B) and Tables 3–4. The only additions over vanilla GRPO are step 4 and step 6.

  1. Sample a batch of tasks

    ALFWorld and WebShop use train batch size 16; Search-QA uses 128. 150 training steps total for every environment.

  2. Retrieve the privileged skill c⁺

    Keyword matching against the SkillBank from SkillRL. One skill per task. Training-only — it never enters the rollout prompt and never enters evaluation.

    SRS = KM (keyword matching)

  3. Roll out G = 8 trajectories, on-policy, unconditioned

    The student generates without the skill. This is the fork from Skill-GRPO, which injects the skill into the training prompt instead. Each turn: prompt → <think> + <action> → environment observation → repeat.

    ALFWorld: ≤50 turns · prompt ≤2048 tok · response ≤512 tok · temp 1.0
    WebShop: ≤15 turns · prompt ≤4096 · Search-QA: ≤4 turns · prompt ≤4096

  4. Verify, then compute the group-relative advantage

    Binary R⁽ⁱ⁾ ∈ {0,1} from the environment verifier. Standardize within the group of 8 to get A_seq⁽ⁱ⁾. The group mean R̄ is retained — it becomes B₀.

  5. One teacher forward pass per trajectory

    Re-score the student's own tokens with c⁺ prepended. Teacher-forced, no sampling. Subtract from the rollout log-probs to get δ_k,t, sum within each turn to get e_k. All of it under stop-gradient.

    This is the entire compute overhead: one forward pass. No extra rollouts, no critic, no learned parameters.

  6. Run the recursion and reshape

    B₀ = clip(R̄, ε, 1−ε) → c_k = γc_{k−1} + e_k → B_k = σ(logit B₀ + c_k) → ΔB_k → q_k = sign(A_seq)ΔB_k → standardize within trajectory → w_k = clip(1 + bz_k, 1±b) → Ã_k = A_seq[(1−λ) + λw_k]. Elementwise, negligible cost.

    λ = 0.5 · b = 0.2 · γ = 0.95 · ε = 1e−4 · shared across all environments and both model scales, no per-task tuning

  7. Policy update

    Standard clipped PPO surrogate, each token carrying its turn's Ã. There is no separate distillation loss. The teacher signal only ever acts through the advantage — that's the design choice separating this from SDAR and Skill-SD, which add auxiliary objectives.

    η = 1e−6 · ε_low/ε_high = 0.2/0.24 (clip-higher) · dual-clip c = 3.0 · KL β = 0.01 · entropy 0.001 · grad clip 1.0 · 1 PPO epoch/update · FSDP, single node 8×H800

Inference

Everything above disappears. {skill context} in the prompt template is left empty, there is no teacher branch, no belief state, no reshaping. It is a plain multi-turn agent loop against the environment at temperature 0.4. Whatever the skills taught has to have been absorbed into the weights during training — which is exactly the claim Skill-GRPO (skill removed at test) versus Skill-GRPO* (skill kept at test) is set up to measure.

Note the asymmetry this creates in Table 1: Skill-GRPO* keeps its privileged context at validation time and still only reaches 88.3 on ALFWorld-7B. AgentOPSD reaches 89.1 with nothing at inference.

Row 3 is the paper's central claim tested directly: swap the recursive revision for the isolated local gap, hold everything else fixed, lose 6.3 points. That's the difference between AgentOPSD and StepOPSD reduced to a controlled comparison — and it is the cleanest evidence that "a local gap is not sequential credit" is more than a slogan.

Rows 4 and 5 are larger, but they're arguably testing more basic things. Losing the sign means credit no longer knows which way the episode went. Losing B₀ means every trajectory starts from an arbitrary point on the sigmoid, mis-scaling the B(1−B) gate for every early turn. Both are load-bearing, neither is the novelty.

Hyperparameter sensitivity

Only λ has a systematic effect: 0.5 is best and every smaller value is worse (89.1 vs 84.4 / 85.9 / 83.6). γ moves results a few points with no monotone trend. ε_high is essentially inert — the reshaped objective inherits GRPO's trust-region robustness, as Prop. 2 would predict. Notably the entire spread collapses on 4-turn Search-QA, which is the method's own consistency check: the knobs that matter on long horizons go quiet when there's no history to accumulate.

JudgmentWhat I'd want before I built on this

The genuinely good parts

  • The core reframe is right and cheaply stated: credit is belief revision, belief revision is recursive, and log-odds is the space where evidence adds. The connection from Bayes factor → sequential probability ratio test → sigmoid-gated TD-like signal is elegant and the propositions are honest one-liners rather than dressing.
  • Sign preservation (Prop. 2) is the right engineering decision. The verifier keeps the direction, the belief only modulates magnitude within ±λb. It cannot manufacture positive signal on a failed episode. That makes it a safe add-on to any group-relative method.
  • The horizon-slope experiment tests the mechanism, not just the leaderboard. More papers should do this.
  • One shared hyperparameter setting across three environments and two model scales, with the sweep published. No per-task tuning.

The parts I'd push on

  • No code. The repo is a README. For a method whose contribution is entirely in the shape of an advantage-reshaping block, the implementation is the paper. Algorithm 1 is detailed enough to reimplement, but nothing is verified.
  • No seeds, no error bars, anywhere in Table 1. Several per-category cells are computed over what look like fewer than 25 episodes (100.0 and 0.0 appear repeatedly). The 0.2-point Search-QA-7B margin cannot be distinguished from noise without variance.
  • Assumption A2 is violated in the operating regime. The Bayes-factor derivation needs success to be rare. On ALFWorld-7B late in training, R̄ is high. The authors retreat to sign-and-ranking, which is defensible — but it means the "Bayesian belief state" is a heuristic accumulator wearing probabilistic clothing, and B_k should not be read as a success probability. They say as much; the framing elsewhere is looser than the caveat.
  • Saturation on long horizons is unaddressed. The B(1−B) gate zeroes out credit once support settles, which on 50-turn ALFWorld episodes means most turns end up indistinguishable and slightly downweighted. γ bounds the accumulator but not below saturation. There is no analysis of what fraction of turns actually receive non-trivial credit.
  • Provenance smell. Figures 4 and 5 are titled "…over training (SABRE)" — a different method name, left in. And "AgentOPSD" is expanded in the title as "Recursive Self-Distillation," which doesn't decompose the acronym at all (OPSD = on-policy self-distillation). Cosmetic, but it suggests a rushed rename over an earlier draft, which is worth knowing when you're weighing how carefully the rest was checked.
  • The history is truncated. The ALFWorld prompt template feeds only the most recent {history length} observations. So s_k in the derivation — nominally the full interaction history — is in practice a window. The recursion partly compensates by carrying evidence forward outside the context, which is arguably a feature, but it's not discussed.
  • Untested against reward-side credit. §4.1 correctly positions GiGPO as complementary (environment rewards vs. distillation evidence) but never runs the combination, or compares head-to-head on the same setup. That's the obvious next experiment.

If you're reproducing it

The reshaping block is maybe forty lines and drops into any GRPO trainer that already tracks turn boundaries. The order that matters: sum δ within a turn before anything else; initialize ℓ₀ from the group mean you already have; apply sign(A_seq) before standardizing, not after; standardize within each trajectory separately, not across the group. Getting the standardization scope wrong is the failure mode that will silently look almost right.
